# Report Structure

Follow this order. It is designed so a worried person reads it without panic and
finishes with something to do tomorrow morning.

1. **The one-paragraph summary.** What the panel says overall, in plain English.
2. **What's going right.** Named explicitly, before any problem. People act from
   hope, not alarm — and they need to know what their protocol must not break.
3. **The one thing to take to a doctor.** Any RED marker, isolated and unmissable,
   with the exact follow-up tests to request.
4. **The pattern.** The causal chain connecting the abnormal markers. One sentence
   per link. This is the heart of the report.
5. **The traditional reading.** TCM pattern, Ayurvedic constitution — named
   specifically, with the classical formulas.
6. **Marker by marker.** Value, range, plain-English meaning, why it is off, and
   the specific correction. Group by system, worst first.
7. **The 4-month protocol, adapted.** Month by month, with every deviation from
   the standard arc stated and justified.
8. **The retest schedule.** What, when, and what number means it worked.
9. **The doctor conversation.** Exact tests to request, written to be handed over.
10. **Start here this week.** Three to five things, beginning tomorrow.

## Rules

- **Every recommendation gets a number.** Not "eat more liver" — "3 oz beef liver,
  twice a week." Not "take vitamin D" — "5,000 IU D3 with your largest fat-
  containing meal, plus 100–200 µg K2 as MK-7." A recommendation without a dose,
  a frequency, and a form is not a recommendation.
- **Every herb gets its caution carried with it**, in the same breath — never in a
  footnote and never in a separate section people skip.
- **Define every term on first use**, inline, in parentheses.
- **Never moralize.** Nobody chose their ferritin.
- **Tables for anything with more than three parallel items.** Prose for reasoning.
- **Say what is uncertain.** A calculated value, a cycle-day-dependent hormone, a
  possibly mistimed draw — flag it rather than reading it as fact.
