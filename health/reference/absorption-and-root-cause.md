# Root-Cause Patterns: Reading a Panel as a System

A lab panel is not a list of independent problems. It is a set of downstream
readouts from a small number of upstream systems. Find the upstream failure and
several markers move at once.

Work this file *before* writing any recommendation.

---

## Pattern 1 — Malabsorption (the most under-diagnosed root cause)

**Signature:** several *chemically unrelated* nutrients low at the same time.
Especially: fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) + minerals (iron, zinc, magnesium)
+ B12/folate (seen as elevated homocysteine) + low total cholesterol.

If someone eats reasonably well and is still low in three unrelated things, the
problem is usually the *gate*, not the *supply*. Supplementing harder into a
closed gate wastes money and time.

### The four gates, in the order they usually fail

**1. Stomach acid (hypochlorhydria).** Acid is required to cleave iron into its
absorbable ferrous form and to release B12 from food protein. Low acid is the
single most common cause of "I take iron and my ferritin doesn't move."
- Causes: age, chronic stress (digestion is parasympathetic — you cannot digest
  in fight-or-flight), H. pylori infection, long-term PPI/antacid use, zinc
  deficiency (zinc is a cofactor for carbonic anhydrase, which makes stomach acid
  — a self-reinforcing loop).
- Traditional fix: **bitters before meals.** Gentian, wormwood, dandelion root,
  artichoke leaf, orange peel. 1–2 mL tincture or a few drops on the tongue
  10–15 minutes pre-meal. The bitter *taste receptors* trigger the cascade — this
  is why bitters must be tasted, not capsuled.
- Also: 1 tbsp raw apple cider vinegar or the juice of half a lemon in a small
  glass of water, 10 minutes before meals.
- Clinical version: betaine HCl with pepsin, titrated with a practitioner.
  **Contraindicated with active ulcer, gastritis, or NSAID/steroid use.**

**2. Bile flow.** Bile emulsifies fat. No bile, no absorption of A/D/E/K, no
cholesterol recycling. Low bile shows up as low fat-soluble vitamins, low total
cholesterol, pale or floating stools, and nausea after fatty meals.
- Traditional fix: bitter greens **daily** — dandelion greens, arugula, radicchio,
  endive, mustard greens. Beets and beet greens. Artichoke.
- Castor oil packs over the right upper abdomen, 30–45 min, 3–4x/week.
- Taurine (500–1000 mg) and ox bile are the clinical versions.

**3. Small intestine integrity.** Celiac disease, SIBO, and inflammatory bowel
disease all blunt the villi where absorption happens.
- **Always screen for celiac (tTG-IgA + total IgA) before accepting "unexplained"
  iron deficiency.** This is a real diagnosis with a real fix and it is missed
  constantly. The test requires eating gluten at the time of testing.
- H. pylori (stool antigen or breath test) — causes both low acid and occult
  blood loss. Two mechanisms, one organism.

**4. Anti-nutrients in the food itself.** Phytic acid in grains, legumes, nuts,
and seeds binds iron, zinc, magnesium, and calcium and carries them out.
Oxalates (spinach, chard, beet greens) bind iron and calcium. Tannins in tea,
coffee, and red wine block non-heme iron by up to 60–90% in the same meal.
- The ancient fix is the *preparation itself*: soaking, sprouting, souring,
  and long fermentation. Sourdough fermentation degrades most phytate; quick-rise
  bread does not. This is why traditional cultures soaked their grains overnight
  and it is the practical core of ancestral nutrition.

## Pattern 2 — Low Energy Availability / Under-fueling

**Signature:** low total cholesterol, low morning cortisol, low-normal glucose and
HbA1c, low ferritin, TSH drifting up, low-normal T3, menstrual changes, low
resting heart rate with poor recovery.

The body reads chronic under-fueling — too few calories, too little fat, too much
training, or all three — as famine, and down-regulates every expensive process:
reproduction, thyroid output, stress response, and tissue repair. Nothing is
"broken"; the system is defending itself.

This is common in active, health-conscious women and is frequently mistaken for a
thyroid or adrenal disease. Under-eating cannot be supplemented around.

**The correction is food volume and dietary fat, not another capsule.** Cholesterol
is the raw substrate for every steroid hormone and for vitamin D. Restricting fat
while asking the body to make more hormones is asking a bakery for bread with no
flour.

## Pattern 3 — Iron Deficiency as the Upstream Driver

Ferritin below ~30 ng/mL is iron deficiency even with a normal hemoglobin and a
normal CBC ("iron deficiency without anemia"). Stores empty long before the blood
count falls — the body protects red cells last.

Downstream effects, all commonly blamed on something else:
- **Thyroid.** Thyroid peroxidase (TPO), the enzyme that builds thyroid hormone,
  is a *heme* enzyme — it needs iron to work. Iron deficiency reduces TPO activity
  substantially, so TSH drifts up. Fix ferritin and TSH often falls without ever
  touching the thyroid.
- **Energy and exercise tolerance.** Iron is in every cytochrome of the
  mitochondrial electron transport chain, not just hemoglobin. Fatigue precedes
  anemia by months.
- **Mood, focus, restless legs.** Iron is the rate-limiting cofactor for tyrosine
  hydroxylase, which makes dopamine.
- **Hair shedding (telogen effluvium)** — associated with ferritin under ~40–50.
- **Cold intolerance** — via both thyroid and circulation.

**Critical:** ferritin is also an acute-phase reactant. Inflammation *raises* it
and can mask a deficiency. Always read ferritin next to hs-CRP. A low ferritin
with a low CRP is unambiguous and real.

**Always ask why.** In a menstruating woman, blood loss is the leading cause, but
"she menstruates" is not a complete workup. Rule out celiac, H. pylori, and GI
bleeding before settling.

## Pattern 4 — HPA-Axis Down-regulation

**Signature:** low morning cortisol, afternoon energy collapse, wired-and-tired at
night, poor stress tolerance, salt cravings, low blood pressure, dizziness on
standing.

Cortisol should *peak* 30–45 minutes after waking (the cortisol awakening
response) and taper all day. A low morning value means either a blunted peak or a
mistimed draw.

**Check the draw time before doing anything else.** A cortisol drawn at 10am is
not comparable to one drawn at 7:30am — the value can halve. Many "low cortisol"
results are timing artifacts.

If genuinely low, the drivers are: chronic under-fueling, chronic sleep debt,
circadian disruption (no morning light, screens at night), overtraining, and
unremitting psychological stress. Also check that cholesterol is adequate —
cortisol is built from it.

**True adrenal insufficiency (Addison's) is a serious medical diagnosis** with
very low cortisol, low sodium, high potassium, and hyperpigmentation. It requires
an ACTH stimulation test, not herbs. Escalate if the picture fits.

## Pattern 5 — Methylation Strain

**Signature:** homocysteine above ~7 µmol/L.

Homocysteine is a crossroads amino acid, cleared by two routes:
- **Remethylation** back to methionine — needs folate (as 5-MTHF), B12, and B2.
- **Transsulfuration** onward to cysteine and glutathione — needs B6 and magnesium.
- **The betaine shortcut** — needs betaine (trimethylglycine), found in beets.

A rising homocysteine means one of those inputs is short. In practice: B12 and
folate first, then B6 and B2. MTHFR gene variants reduce the efficiency of
converting folic acid to usable 5-MTHF, which is why the methylated forms are
preferred.

Note the loop back to Pattern 1: **B12 absorption requires stomach acid and
intrinsic factor.** Elevated homocysteine alongside low iron is a strong hint that
the stomach, not the diet, is the problem.

## Pattern 6 — Subclinical Thyroid Under-function

**Signature:** TSH above ~2.0–2.5 µIU/mL with a "normal" reading, plus fatigue,
cold hands and feet, dry skin, constipation, hair thinning, slow morning start.

TSH is the *pituitary's complaint*, not thyroid output — it rises as the pituitary
shouts louder at an underperforming gland. It moves first, before T4 and T3 fall
out of range, which is why a TSH of 3–4 with "normal" labs so often accompanies
real symptoms.

Thyroid hormone production requires, at minimum: **iodine** (the atom itself),
**tyrosine** (the backbone), **iron** (TPO's heme), **selenium** (deiodinase,
which converts T4 to active T3, and glutathione peroxidase, which protects the
gland), **zinc**, and **vitamin A**.

A "thyroid problem" is very often a nutrient problem wearing a thyroid mask.

**Note the cystatin C link:** hypothyroidism *raises* serum cystatin C
independently of kidney function. An elevated cystatin C alongside an elevated TSH
should prompt thyroid evaluation before concluding kidney disease.
