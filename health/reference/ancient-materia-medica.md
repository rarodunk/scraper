# Ancient Materia Medica

Traditional remedies with their classical indication, modern mechanism where one
exists, real dosing, and real cautions. Never recommend from this file without
carrying the caution line with it.

**Legend:** [T] traditional use only · [T+] traditional use with supporting modern
evidence · [M] modern evidence-led.

---

## I. Blood Building (TCM: Xue Xu / Ayurveda: Pandu Roga / rakta dhatu depletion)

The traditional category for what a modern lab calls iron deficiency. Every
literate medical tradition had one, and they converge remarkably.

### Traditional Chinese Medicine

**Si Wu Tang — "Four Substance Decoction."** The foundational blood tonic of
Chinese medicine, in continuous use for roughly a thousand years. Four herbs: [T+]
- *Dang Gui* (Angelica sinensis, "female ginseng") — the chief blood tonic; also
  moves blood and is used for menstrual regulation.
- *Shu Di Huang* (prepared Rehmannia glutinosa) — nourishes blood and yin.
- *Bai Shao* (Paeonia lactiflora, white peony) — softens the liver, relieves cramping.
- *Chuan Xiong* (Ligusticum chuanxiong) — moves blood so the tonics don't stagnate.

**Ba Zhen Tang — "Eight Treasure Decoction."** Si Wu Tang combined with Si Jun Zi
Tang (the qi tonic). The correct choice when blood deficiency comes *with* fatigue
and weak digestion — i.e. when you must build the blood and repair the absorption
at the same time. [T]

**Gui Pi Tang — "Restore the Spleen Decoction."** For blood deficiency presenting
with fatigue, anxiety, overthinking, palpitations, and poor sleep. [T]

**Huang Qi** (Astragalus membranaceus) — the great qi tonic; taken as a decoction
or simmered whole in soup and broth, which is the traditional domestic method. [T+]

> **Cautions.** Dang Gui has anticoagulant activity — **avoid with warfarin and
> other blood thinners, and discontinue 2 weeks before surgery.** Avoid Dang Gui
> in pregnancy and during heavy menstrual flow. *He Shou Wu* (Polygonum
> multiflorum) appears in many blood-building formulas and **has documented cases
> of severe hepatotoxicity — do not recommend it.** Source Chinese herbs only from
> suppliers doing third-party heavy-metal testing.

### Ayurveda

Iron deficiency is *pandu roga* ("the pallor disease"), described in the Charaka
Samhita with a treatment that is essentially iron plus vitamin C plus digestive
repair — arrived at roughly two thousand years early.

- **Amalaki / Amla** (Emblica officinalis) — among the densest whole-food vitamin C
  sources known, and unusually heat-stable. Classically paired *with* iron
  preparations, which is precisely the ascorbate-enhances-iron-absorption
  mechanism. 1 tsp powder in warm water daily, or 1–2 fresh fruits. [T+]
- **Punarnavadi Mandur / Dhatri Lauha** — classical iron-bearing preparations for
  pandu roga. **Only under a qualified Ayurvedic physician**; traditional *bhasma*
  and *mandur* preparations have documented heavy-metal contamination risk in
  commercial products.
- **Shatavari** (Asparagus racemosus) — the principal female tonic; builds ojas
  and supports lactation and cycle regularity. 500–1000 mg twice daily. [T+]
- **Triphala** — three fruits (amalaki, bibhitaki, haritaki); the universal
  digestive and elimination tonic. 1/2–1 tsp in warm water at bedtime. [T+]
- **Punarnava** (Boerhavia diffusa) — the classical *kidney* herb, used for fluid
  retention and renal support. [T]

### Western and folk herbalism

- **Nettle leaf** (Urtica dioica) — the mineral-dense herb of Western practice.
  **Preparation is everything:** a 4-hour to overnight *long infusion* (1 oz dried
  leaf in 1 quart just-boiled water, covered) extracts far more mineral content
  than a 5-minute teabag. 1–2 cups daily. [T+]
- **Yellow dock root** (Rumex crispus) — the classic folk iron tonic. Works two
  ways: it contains some iron, and as a bitter it stimulates acid and bile,
  improving absorption of iron from everything else in the meal. Decoction or
  tincture, 1–2 mL up to 3x daily. Mildly laxative. [T]
- **Dandelion root and leaf** (Taraxacum officinale) — bitter, bile-moving,
  mineral-rich; root roasted as a coffee substitute, leaf in salad. [T+]
- **Red raspberry leaf** (Rubus idaeus) — the traditional women's uterine tonic,
  mineral-rich as a long infusion. [T]
- **Blackstrap molasses** — the genuine folk iron food; 1 tbsp delivers roughly
  1–2 mg iron plus magnesium and potassium. Non-heme, so pair with vitamin C.
  High in sugar — a supplement, not a staple. [T]
- **Floradix / Floravital** — a modern liquid herbal iron tonic in the European
  tradition (ferrous gluconate in an herb-and-fruit base); notably gentle on the
  gut. [M]
- **Cooking in cast iron** — a genuinely measurable ancient method. Acidic,
  moisture-rich foods simmered in bare cast iron (tomato sauce, stews, chili) leach
  meaningful iron into the food. Free, and it works. [T+]

### Ancestral food practice
- **Liver** — the most iron- and nutrient-dense food available. 3 oz beef liver:
  roughly 5 mg highly-absorbable heme iron, ~50 µg B12, ~200 µg folate, plus
  retinol and copper. Traditional in essentially every food culture on earth.
- **Oysters and shellfish** — 3 oz canned oysters: ~6–8 mg iron plus very high
  zinc, copper, and B12.
- **Bone broth** — long-simmered with a splash of vinegar to draw minerals out of
  the bone; glycine and gelatin support gut lining repair, which closes the loop
  back to absorption.
- **Ferments** — sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir, yogurt. Lactic acid fermentation
  degrades phytate and increases mineral bioavailability, and the acidity itself
  aids iron uptake. **Natto** is the densest known source of vitamin K2 (MK-7).

## II. Adrenal and HPA Support (TCM: Kidney Yang / Ayurveda: Vata + low ojas)

- **Ashwagandha** (Withania somnifera) — the premier Ayurvedic *rasayana*
  (rejuvenative). Best-evidenced adaptogen for cortisol modulation and perceived
  stress. 300–600 mg/day of a root extract standardized to ~5% withanolides.
  Take in the evening if it is used for sleep and stress. [T+]
  > **Cautions:** nightshade family. May *increase* thyroid hormone output — good
  > if hypothyroid, risky if hyperthyroid. Avoid in pregnancy. Rare hepatotoxicity
  > reports. Caution with autoimmune disease and with sedatives.

- **Licorice root** (Glycyrrhiza glabra / Yashtimadhu / Gan Cao) — the traditional
  remedy for low cortisol, and it has a real mechanism: glycyrrhizin metabolites
  inhibit 11β-HSD2, the enzyme that inactivates cortisol into cortisone,
  effectively extending the half-life of the cortisol you already make. [T+]
  > **Cautions — this is the highest-risk herb in this file.** That same mechanism
  > produces pseudoaldosteronism: sodium and fluid retention, **hypertension, and
  > hypokalemia (low potassium), which can be life-threatening.** Risk rises with
  > dose and duration. **Do not use with high blood pressure, heart disease,
  > kidney disease, or diuretics. Do not use in pregnancy.** If used at all: only
  > with a practitioner, whole root as a morning tea, limited to roughly 4–6 weeks
  > with blood pressure and potassium monitored. **DGL (deglycyrrhizinated)
  > licorice has this compound removed — it is safe for the gut but does nothing
  > for cortisol.**

- **Holy basil / Tulsi** (Ocimum sanctum) — "the incomparable one," the sacred
  adaptogen of Ayurveda. Gentle, safe as a daily tea, 1–3 cups. [T+]
- **Rhodiola** (Rhodiola rosea) — Siberian/Scandinavian tradition for fatigue and
  stress endurance. 200–400 mg standardized extract, **morning only** — it is
  stimulating and will disrupt sleep if taken late. [T+]
- **Eleuthero** (Eleutherococcus senticosus), **Cordyceps**, **Reishi**
  (Ganoderma lucidum) — Kidney-tonifying in TCM; reishi is calming and evening-
  appropriate. [T+]
- **Bone broth with sea salt** — the traditional restorative for the low-cortisol,
  low-blood-pressure picture; sodium is genuinely needed when aldosterone is low.

## III. Thyroid Support

The gland is a factory; these are its raw materials.
- **Iodine** — from **sea vegetables**: kombu, wakame, nori, dulse. The ancient
  coastal-diet solution. **Kelp is extremely iodine-dense and easy to overdose;
  excess iodine can trigger or worsen thyroid disease, especially autoimmune
  thyroiditis.** Prefer 1–2 sheets of nori or a small piece of wakame in soup a
  few times weekly over high-dose kelp tablets.
- **Selenium** — **2 Brazil nuts daily is the entire dose.** ~70–90 µg each; the
  tolerable upper limit is 400 µg/day and selenium toxicity is real (hair loss,
  brittle nails, garlic breath). Do not eat handfuls.
- **Zinc** — oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds. 15–30 mg/day supplemental maximum;
  **always pair long-term zinc with 1–2 mg copper**, as zinc depletes copper.
- **Tyrosine** — the amino-acid backbone; from complete protein.
- **Guggulu** (Commiphora mukul) — the classical Ayurvedic thyroid and lipid herb.
  [T] **Interacts with many drugs; avoid in pregnancy.**
- **Avoid raw goitrogens in volume** (raw kale, cabbage, broccoli in daily large
  smoothies) when iodine is marginal. **Cooking largely deactivates them** — this
  is why traditional cultures fermented or cooked their brassicas.

## IV. Practices (free, ancient, and often the highest-leverage item)

- **Morning sunlight, 10–20 minutes within an hour of waking, no sunglasses.**
  Anchors the cortisol awakening response and the entire circadian cascade
  downstream of it. This is the single highest-return intervention for a blunted
  morning cortisol, and it costs nothing.
- **Midday sun on bare skin** — the only meaningful natural source of vitamin D.
  10–30 min depending on skin tone and latitude, arms and legs exposed, before
  burning. Traditional *heliotherapy*.
- **Abhyanga** — Ayurvedic self-massage with warm sesame oil before bathing.
  Deeply vata-pacifying and parasympathetic; excellent for the HPA picture.
- **Pranayama, especially nadi shodhana** (alternate-nostril breathing), 5–10 min.
  Shifts autonomic balance toward parasympathetic — the state in which digestion
  and absorption actually occur.
- **Eat in a calm, seated state.** Ancient across every tradition, and mechanistic:
  stomach acid and bile release are parasympathetic. Eating while stressed or
  rushed measurably reduces what you extract from the meal.
- **Qi Gong, tai chi, restorative yoga, yoga nidra** — build capacity without the
  cortisol cost of hard training, which matters when the HPA axis is depleted.
- **Sauna and sweat lodge; contrast hydrotherapy.** Cardiovascular and
  detoxification traditions across Finnish, Native American, Roman, and Russian
  cultures.
- **Seasonal and circadian eating** — largest meal at midday when digestive fire
  (agni) peaks; light early dinner. Ayurvedic, and it aligns with modern
  chrononutrition findings.
- **Castor oil packs** over the liver — folk practice for bile flow and
  elimination. 30–45 minutes with gentle heat, 3–4x weekly.
