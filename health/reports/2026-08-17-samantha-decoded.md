## The one thing to take to a doctor

### Cystatin C — 1.17 mg/L (optimal 0.55–0.9; the lab's widest range stops at 1.01)

This is the only marker on your panel that fell outside **every** range the lab
provides, and it is the only one I would put on a calendar.

**What it is:** a small protein your cells release at a steady rate that your
kidneys filter out. Because production is constant, the level in your blood
estimates how well your kidneys are filtering. It is often a *better* kidney
marker than creatinine because it is not thrown off by muscle mass.

**Why I am not alarmed, and why you should still get it checked:** cystatin C is
raised by several things that have nothing to do with kidney function —
**an underactive thyroid, corticosteroid use, inflammation, obesity, smoking, and
simple dehydration at the time of the draw.**

Look at what else is on your panel. **Your TSH is 2.78 — an early
under-functioning thyroid — and low thyroid function is documented to raise
cystatin C independently of the kidneys.** Your CRP is 0.24, so inflammation is
not the cause. That gives a plausible, benign, *and fixable* explanation for this
number sitting right where it is.

But "plausible" is not "confirmed," and a single elevated value is never a
diagnosis. **Book this. Do not wait four months for a protocol to finish.**

**Ask for exactly this:**
1. Repeat **cystatin C**, plus **serum creatinine, BUN, and electrolytes** — drawn
   well-hydrated. If creatinine is normal while cystatin C is high, the non-kidney
   explanations move to the front.
2. **eGFR calculated both ways** — by creatinine and by cystatin C.
3. **Urine albumin:creatinine ratio (ACR)** — the earliest sign of true kidney
   stress, and it is a simple urine cup.
4. **Full thyroid panel** — free T3, free T4, reverse T3, TPO and thyroglobulin
   antibodies — to test the thyroid explanation directly.
5. Mention any **NSAID use** (ibuprofen, naproxen) or any recent **steroid**
   course, including inhaled or topical. Both move this number.

**Until it is cleared: no aggressive detox, no high-dose herbal protocols, no
sustained very-high-protein intake, and go easy on NSAIDs.** Herbs and binders are
cleared through the liver and kidneys, and you do not load an organ whose function
is still a question mark. This is the single biggest reason your protocol below is
sequenced the way it is.

## The pattern: eleven numbers, two root causes

This is the part that matters. Your abnormal markers are not eleven separate
problems — they are a chain. Read it once and the whole plan makes sense.

**Root cause A — you are not absorbing well.**

Look at what is low: **iron** (a mineral), **vitamin D** (fat-soluble),
**cholesterol** (fat), and **B12/folate** (shown by your homocysteine of 8.13).
Those are chemically unrelated. When several unrelated nutrients are low at the
same time in someone who eats reasonably well, the problem is usually **the gate,
not the supply** — and taking more supplements through a closed gate is how people
spend two years and a lot of money without moving a number.

The usual gates, in the order they fail: **low stomach acid** (needed to make iron
absorbable and to release B12 from food), **low bile flow** (needed for every
fat-soluble vitamin and for cholesterol), and **anti-nutrients** — the tannins in
tea and coffee, which block up to 60–90% of the iron in the same meal, and the
phytates in unsoaked grains and legumes.

**Root cause B — you are under-fuelled, particularly on fat.**

Low total cholesterol, low morning cortisol, low ferritin, and a low-normal A1c
form a recognizable cluster. The body reads chronic under-fuelling as famine and
turns down everything expensive: hormone production, thyroid output, stress
response, repair.

**Here is the detail that convinced me.** An underactive thyroid normally pushes
cholesterol *up*. Your TSH says early under-function, and your cholesterol is 111
anyway — **something is pulling it down hard enough to overcome the thyroid
pushing it up.** That points squarely at intake and absorption.

### The chain

1. **Low iron → your thyroid.** Thyroid peroxidase (TPO), the enzyme that
   actually builds thyroid hormone, is a **heme enzyme — it requires iron to
   function.** Iron deficiency sharply reduces its activity. Your pituitary
   notices output falling and shouts louder: **TSH rises to 2.78.**
2. **Low thyroid → your cystatin C.** Low thyroid function raises cystatin C
   independently of kidney function. **This is likely why 1.17 appeared.**
3. **Low cholesterol → your cortisol.** Cortisol is built from cholesterol, via
   pregnenolone. Total cholesterol 111 means thin raw material, and
   **morning cortisol comes in at 6.6.**
4. **Low cholesterol → your vitamin D.** Your skin makes vitamin D from
   7-dehydrocholesterol — a cholesterol molecule — using UVB. Less substrate,
   less D, even with the same sun. **42.85 instead of 50–80.**
5. **Low stomach acid → your homocysteine.** B12 absorption needs stomach acid and
   intrinsic factor. The same closed gate blocking your iron is plausibly
   blocking your B12, and homocysteine rises to **8.13** when B12 and folate run
   short.
6. **Low iron and low fuel → your energy, hair, temperature, and mood** — through
   every one of the above at once.

**The leverage point is iron and fat.** Correct those two properly and there is a
real chance the thyroid number, the cystatin C, the cortisol, and the vitamin D
all move without ever being treated directly.

### Ferritin — 21.1 ng/mL (optimal 70–90) — **the priority**

**What it is:** your body's *stored* iron. If serum iron is the checking account,
ferritin is the savings account. Stores empty long before a blood count changes —
which is why you can be genuinely iron deficient with a perfectly normal CBC, and
why fatigue can precede anemia by many months.

**Yours is at 30% of optimal, and below 30 is deficient by any standard.**

Ferritin also rises with inflammation, which can mask a deficiency. **Your CRP is
0.24 — so this reading is clean.** It is real.

**The symptoms this explains** — and they are usually blamed on stress or age:
fatigue that sleep does not fix, hair shedding (associated with ferritin under
~40–50), cold hands and feet, brain fog, breathlessness on stairs, restless legs
at night, brittle nails, low mood and anxiety, and poor exercise tolerance. Iron
is also the rate-limiting cofactor for the enzyme that makes **dopamine**, which
is why low ferritin so often reads as low motivation.

**Why it happened — and "she menstruates" is not a complete answer.** Monthly

**Do not treat this as a thyroid problem yet.** It is very likely a *nutrient*

### Homocysteine — 8.13 µmol/L (optimal 5–7)

**Beets are the single best food lever** — they supply betaine (trimethylglycine),

### LDL-C:ApoB Ratio — 0.85 (optimal 1.5–2.0)

**Read this one with real caution — I think it is substantially an artifact.**

artery walls more easily.

ratio.

**And the number that actually predicts risk is fine.** ApoB is 59, comfortably

predictor there is. **Low particle count means low risk regardless of how they are
packed.**

**Verify rather than worry:** ask for a **direct LDL** or an **NMR LipoProfile**.

### Estradiol 66.8 & FSH 3.07 — now interpretable, and I need to correct myself

**Estradiol 66.8 is unremarkable for cycle day −1.** Estradiol has a secondary
rise mid-luteal and then falls as the corpus luteum regresses premenstrually.
66.8 the day before bleeding is a normal, expected value. It also means this
number tells you nothing about your follicular-phase estrogen.

# The Supplement Stack

Every item: form, dose, timing, and what to keep it away from. Forms matter —
they are the difference between a supplement working and passing through.

### Iron — the priority
> **Ferrous bisglycinate (chelated), 25–50 mg elemental iron, EVERY OTHER
> MORNING, on an empty stomach, with 250–500 mg vitamin C.**

- **Alternate days, not daily.** A dose raises hepcidin — the hormone that shuts
  iron absorption down — for ~24 hours, returning to baseline by 48. Alternate-day
  dosing absorbs roughly **twice** the total iron of daily dosing. A single morning
  dose also beats split doses.
- **Bisglycinate**, not ferrous sulfate — dramatically gentler on the gut, which is
  why most people quit iron.
- **Gentler alternative:** *Floradix / Floravital* liquid herbal iron tonic — lower
  potency, very well tolerated, in the European herbal tradition.
- **Keep 2 hours away from:** coffee, tea, calcium and dairy, zinc and magnesium
  supplements, and antacids.
- **Take with:** vitamin C, or amla, or a squeeze of lemon.
- ⚠️ **Never supplement iron long-term without confirmed deficiency and retesting.**
  Iron overload damages liver, heart and pancreas. **Stop if ferritin exceeds 150.**
  Iron is also the leading cause of fatal poisoning in young children — **store it
  locked and out of reach.**

### Vitamin D — with its three cofactors
> **D3 (cholecalciferol) 5,000 IU daily, with your largest fat-containing meal.**
> **Plus vitamin K2 (MK-7) 100–200 µg** and **magnesium glycinate 200–400 mg.**

Never take D alone. **D increases calcium absorption; K2 directs that calcium into
bone rather than into arteries.** Magnesium is required to convert D into its
active form — a stalled vitamin D level is very often a magnesium problem.
**Retest at 12 weeks. Do not exceed 10,000 IU/day without monitoring** — vitamin D
is fat-soluble and accumulates.

### Methylation support — for homocysteine
> **Methylated B-complex daily**, providing roughly: methylfolate 400–800 µg,
> methylcobalamin (B12) 1,000 µg, P5P (B6) 25–50 mg, riboflavin (B2) 25–50 mg.

Methylated forms, not folic acid and cyanocobalamin. Add **beets or beet juice
daily** for betaine. Retest homocysteine at 12 weeks, targeting under 7.

### Omega-3
> **EPA/DHA 2,000–3,000 mg daily with food.** Or **fermented cod liver oil** — the
> traditional choice, which brings vitamin A alongside D. Raises HDL, rebuilds
> membranes, supports particle size.

### Thyroid raw materials
> **Selenium: exactly 2 Brazil nuts daily.** That is the whole dose (~140–180 µg).
> **Do not eat handfuls** — the upper limit is 400 µg/day and selenium toxicity is
> real (hair loss, brittle nails, garlic breath).
>
> **Zinc 15–25 mg with food, plus 1–2 mg copper** — zinc depletes copper over time,
> always pair them.
>
> **Iodine — gently, from food.** 1–2 sheets of nori, or a small piece of wakame in
> soup, 2–3x/week. ⚠️ **Avoid high-dose kelp tablets.** Excess iodine can trigger
> or worsen thyroid disease, especially autoimmune thyroiditis — and you do not yet
> know your antibody status. Food-level iodine only until you do.

### Adaptogens — for cortisol
> **Ashwagandha (root extract, ~5% withanolides) 300–600 mg, evening.**

Axe's stated first choice for adrenals *and* thyroid, and the best-evidenced
adaptogen for cortisol modulation.
⚠️ **Nightshade family. May increase thyroid hormone output** — likely helpful
given your TSH, but it means you should retest thyroid rather than assume. **Avoid
in pregnancy.** Caution with autoimmune conditions and sedatives; rare liver injury
reports.

> **Holy basil (tulsi) tea, 1–3 cups daily.** Gentle, safe, pleasant — the sacred
> adaptogen of Ayurveda. Good afternoon replacement for a second coffee.

⚠️ **Licorice root: practitioner-supervised only.** See the caution in the cortisol
section above. Real mechanism, real risk.

### Traditional formulas — with a practitioner
- **TCM: *Ba Zhen Tang*** (Eight Treasure) — blood and qi together, the precise
  classical match for your pattern. Via a licensed TCM herbalist, third-party
  tested. **Not with blood thinners; not in pregnancy.**
- **Ayurveda: Shatavari** 500–1000 mg twice daily (female tonic, builds ojas);
  **Amla** 1 tsp powder daily (vitamin C, classically paired with iron);
  **Triphala** ½–1 tsp at bedtime (agni and elimination).
  ⚠️ **Avoid traditional *bhasma* and *mandur* iron preparations** unless prescribed
  by a qualified Ayurvedic physician — commercial products carry documented
  heavy-metal contamination risk.
- **Western: nettle leaf long infusion** — 1 oz dried leaf in 1 quart just-boiled
  water, covered, steeped **4 hours to overnight**, 1–2 cups daily. **The long
  steep is the entire point** — a 5-minute teabag extracts a fraction of the
  minerals.

### The absorption rules — free, and worth as much as the supplements
1. **Vitamin C with every plant-iron meal.** Amla, bell pepper, citrus, or 250–500
   mg ascorbic acid. It converts iron to its absorbable form — the pairing Ayurveda
   worked out two thousand years ago.
2. **No coffee or tea within 2 hours of iron.** Up to 60–90% blocked otherwise.
3. **A little meat with plant-iron meals** raises absorption of the plant iron
   too — the "meat factor," and the reason traditional cuisines pair lentils with
   a small amount of meat.
4. **Soak, sprout or sour grains and legumes.** Phytate binds iron and zinc and
   carries them out. Sourdough fermentation degrades it; quick-rise bread does not.
5. **Cook acidic simmered foods in bare cast iron** — tomato sauce, chili, stew.
   Genuinely measurable, and free.
6. **Calcium and dairy away from iron by 2 hours.**
7. **Eat seated and calm.** Digestion is parasympathetic.

# The Doctor Conversation

Hand this section over. It is written to be read by a clinician.

**Priority — within 2 weeks:**
1. **Repeat cystatin C, plus serum creatinine, BUN, electrolytes**, drawn
   well-hydrated. **eGFR by both equations.** **Urine albumin:creatinine ratio.**
2. **Full thyroid panel** — free T3, free T4, reverse T3, **TPO and thyroglobulin
   antibodies**. Both to work up the TSH of 2.78 and to test whether low thyroid
   function explains the cystatin C.

**Iron workup — before starting long-term supplementation:**

3. **Full iron panel** — serum iron, TIBC, transferrin saturation — plus **CBC with
   MCV and RDW**.
4. **Celiac screen — tTG-IgA plus total IgA.** Must be eating gluten at the time
   of the test. **Do not skip this**; it is the most commonly missed cause of
   unexplained iron deficiency.
5. **H. pylori** — stool antigen or urea breath test. Causes both low stomach acid
   and occult bleeding.
6. **If periods are heavy** — a gynaecological conversation. Ferritin of 21 in a
   menstruating woman is often menstrual loss, and that is treatable in its own
   right.

**Supporting:**
7. **B12, serum folate, and methylmalonic acid (MMA)** — MMA is more sensitive than
   serum B12 for true tissue deficiency.
8. **Repeat morning cortisol at 7:30–8am, within 30–60 min of waking** — the
   original was drawn at 9am, inside the standard window, so the 6.6 should be
   treated as probably real. Note the wake time on the requisition. A 4-point
   salivary or DUTCH curve is better still because it is anchored to waking. If
   confirmed low, discuss whether an **ACTH stimulation test** is warranted.
9. **Direct LDL or NMR LipoProfile** — to resolve the calculated LDL-C:ApoB ratio.
10. **Mid-luteal progesterone (~7 days before the period is due, roughly 7–8
    Sept)** — to confirm ovulation and to check the third hormone downstream of
    the same cholesterol-derived precursor pool as cortisol.
11. **Day-3 FSH, LH and estradiol on the next period (~17 Sept).** The 17 August
    FSH of 3.07 was drawn on cycle day −1, when FSH is physiologically suppressed,
    and cannot be used to assess ovarian reserve.

**Also mention:** any NSAID or steroid use, all current supplements, training load,
and whether you have been eating at a deficit.

### A last word

Nothing on this panel is frightening, and none of it is your fault. Your heart and
metabolic markers are genuinely excellent — that is the hard part, and you already
have it. What is left is **depletion**, and depletion responds to food.

absorption rules and the food are doing more of the work than the capsules are.**

first.** That is the whole reasoning behind the two changes made to the four-month
plan, and it is the one idea worth carrying out of this document.
